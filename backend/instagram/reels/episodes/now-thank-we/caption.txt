He wrote this thank-you hymn. Then plague took his wife.

Martin Rinkart was a pastor in Eilenburg, a small walled town in Saxony, Germany, from 1617 until his death in 1649. Around 1630, with the Thirty Years’ War already raging, he wrote “Nun danket alle Gott” (“Now Thank We All Our God”) as a short prayer for the table; it was first printed in 1636. From the early 1630s, armies and refugees crowded into the town, and in 1637 plague killed about 4,000 people there, among them his wife, Christine. For a time he was the only pastor left; tradition says he led as many as 40 or 50 funerals a day. His biographers write that he met the war and his grief with trust in God, and he served the town through the whole war. He lived to see the war end in 1648. Nearly 400 years later, churches still sing his table prayer at Thanksgiving. A hymn story about giving thanks in a hard year.
