An angel said his prayer was heard. He asked for proof.

Zechariah was a priest, and he and his wife Elizabeth were “both righteous before God,” but they had no child, and they were old. One day he was chosen by lot to burn incense in the temple, an honor a priest received once in a lifetime, if ever (Mishnah, Tamid 5:2). An angel told him, “Your request has been heard” (Luke 1:13). He asked, “How can I be sure of this?” His sign: he could not speak until the promise came true (Luke 1:20). Then he wrote, “His name is John,” and his first words were praise. His own name, Zechariah, means “the Lord has remembered.” God had, even in the years with no answer. A Bible story about waiting on a prayer, from Luke 1.

Their son grew up to be John the Baptist, who prepared the way for Jesus. Zechariah’s song (Luke 1:68–79) is still prayed every morning in many churches. It ends with the verse below.

“…the dawn from on high will visit us, to shine on those who sit in darkness and the shadow of death; to guide our feet into the way of peace.”
Luke 1:78–79 (WEB)
