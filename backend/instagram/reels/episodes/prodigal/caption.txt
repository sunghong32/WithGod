The Return of the Prodigal Son: the story behind Rembrandt’s painting.

Rembrandt painted it near the end of his life, about 1668. It shows a moment from a story Jesus told in Luke 15. A son who wasted everything comes home hoping only to be hired as a servant, and his father runs to meet him.

Look closely and the father’s hands don’t match. One is broad and strong, the other slender and gentle. Henri Nouwen, who wrote a book about this painting, saw a father’s hand and a mother’s hand. The son has one bare foot, its sandal slipped off, and the other sandal is worn through. In the story, the father calls for the best robe, a ring, and sandals for his feet. On the right stands the older brother. He wouldn’t come in, so the father went out to him too.
A Bible story about coming home, from Luke 15.

“…this, my son, was dead and is alive again. He was lost and is found.”
