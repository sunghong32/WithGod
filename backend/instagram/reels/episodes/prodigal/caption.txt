Rembrandt once painted himself as the prodigal son. Thirty years later, he painted this.

In about 1635, young Rembrandt painted himself and his wife Saskia as the prodigal son in a tavern. Over the next thirty years he lost Saskia, four of his children, and his house in bankruptcy. Near the end of his life, he painted The Return of the Prodigal Son.

In the story Jesus told in Luke 15, a son wastes everything and comes home hoping only to be taken back as a servant. Before he can ask, his father holds him.

Look at the father’s hands. One is broad and strong, the other slender and gentle. Henri Nouwen, who wrote a book about this painting, saw a father’s hand that holds and a mother’s hand that comforts. The Bible speaks of God this way too: “As one whom his mother comforts, so I will comfort you.” (Isaiah 66:13)

“But while he was still far off, his father saw him and was moved with compassion, and ran, fell on his neck, and kissed him.”
