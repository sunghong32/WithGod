The father’s hands in this painting don’t match.

In Rembrandt’s The Return of the Prodigal Son (about 1668), one hand on the son’s back is broad and strong, the other slender and gentle. Henri Nouwen, who wrote a book about this painting, saw a father’s hand and a mother’s hand. The son kneels with a shaved head and torn clothes, one foot bare, its sandal slipped off. On the right stands the older brother. In Jesus’ story he was angry and wouldn’t come in, so the father went out to him too. The whole story is in Luke 15:11–32.
