The father’s hands in this painting don’t match.

4 details most people miss in Rembrandt’s The Return of the Prodigal Son (about 1668):

1. The hands. One is broad and strong, the other slender and gentle. Henri Nouwen, who wrote a book about this painting, saw a father’s hand and a mother’s hand.
2. The sword. The son has lost everything, but a short sword still hangs at his hip. Some see it as the last sign he was still a son.
3. The feet. One sandal has slipped off, the other is worn through. In the story, the father’s first order is a ring for his hand and sandals for his feet.
4. The man on the right. Most see the older brother. Angry, he wouldn’t come in, so the father went out to him too. Jesus never says if he went in.

The whole story is in Luke 15:11–32.
